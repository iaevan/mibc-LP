# Design System: Beyond the Ordinary — MIBC Recruitment

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Beyond the Ordinary"**

The completed system presents MIBC through five distinct recruitment worlds. Burgundy, crimson, warm paper, fine geometric linework, and expressive typography connect them; their compositions and materials stay individual. The voice is curious, creative, ambitious, and welcoming. The title is original proposal copy, not an official event name.

Signal is a cinematic campaign with one sculptural image. Edition is an ivory student publication. Orbit is a dark interactive atlas. Studio is a coral creative canvas. Invitation is a burgundy invitation beside a warm paper letter. All share a readable club identity, clear application action, native scrolling, and restrained feedback. Apple Design and Impeccable inform the physical response, legibility, and finishing restraint already present in the code.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Five distinct page worlds with shared foundations.
- Large balanced headings, serif emphasis, generous margins, and fine rules.
- Geometric linework, asterisks, simple arrows, and one crimson sculpture.
- Direct application actions and native expandable answers.
- Scroll-controlled and finite motion, local assets, and preference-aware presentation.

### Neutral

The fixed layout navigator has a shared translucent dark ground, cream text, muted numbering, and a light selected capsule. Its opaque ground is used for reduced transparency. Each route’s `*-line` color separates sections and native details without a card background. Use the matched route’s muted color for supporting copy; the increased-contrast preference promotes muted text and rules to the current ink.

## Typography

**Body Font:** locally hosted DM Sans Variable, with sans-serif fallback. **Graphic Font:** locally hosted Space Grotesk Variable, with sans-serif fallback. **Serif Font:** locally hosted Instrument Serif, with serif fallback, in separate normal and italic files. Every font uses swap; DM Sans and the italic serif are preloaded. The root size is (16px), with optical sizing enabled.

DM Sans carries Signal and the reading-focused pages. Space Grotesk carries the wordmark and the full Orbit and Studio worlds. Instrument Serif gives Edition and Invitation their headline voice and supplies italic emphasis elsewhere. Orbit’s hero and about emphasis stays upright Space Grotesk; some later emphasis uses the shared serif treatment.

The five `display-*` roles in frontmatter record desktop hero type. The common `headline` is the base section heading; route overrides are intentional. Body copy ranges from (.8–1rem), the shared lead is (1.35rem / 1.5), FAQ questions are (.92rem), and small editorial captions use smaller, widely spaced type. Heading weights are normally (500), Signal’s hero is (450), and Studio’s hero and graphic titles are (650). Instrument Serif files themselves contain weight (400). Headings balance wrapping; paragraphs keep their natural reading flow. Text measures vary by role, typically (48–65ch) for reading passages and (33ch) in journey summaries.

Mobile hero sizes are independently tuned: Signal (`clamp(3.9rem, 11.3vw, 6.4rem)`), Edition (`clamp(3.8rem, 12vw, 6rem)`), Orbit (`clamp(3rem, 9.6vw, 5rem)`), Studio (`clamp(3rem, 9.8vw, 5.8rem)`), and Invitation (`clamp(4rem, 12vw, 6rem)`). Do not replace those with one shared mobile headline size.

## Layout

The common page gutter is `max(5.6vw, calc((100vw - 1440px) / 2))`; below the mobile breakpoint it becomes (6vw). The header and footer use (5.6vw), adopting the wide-screen centering rule above (1600px). The header is (112px) tall by default, (100px) for Edition and Invitation, and (88px) on mobile. The body supports a minimum width of (320px). Major passages use generous vertical padding, commonly the `section-small`, `section-medium`, and `section-large` spacing tokens.

Desktop composition remains route-specific: Signal’s copy takes (62%) beside a sculpture; Edition’s hero uses a (1.05fr / 1fr) grid and its contents use four columns; Orbit centers a (580px) globe at a maximum (70%) width; Studio layers rotated paper artifacts beside large type; Invitation uses a (46% / 54%) split with a sticky left panel and a reading column padded (12%). Invitation’s panel fills at least (780px) or the viewport height.

At (1100px) and below, the long brand expansion disappears, artwork and gutters tighten, the globe narrows, and Invitation’s inner padding becomes (10%). At (760px) and below, split layouts stack, shared FAQ and journey grids become single-column, Edition contents become two columns, artwork enters normal flow, and Invitation’s panel stops sticking; its reading paper uses (8%) padding. Signal hides the discipline summary column while retaining headings, and Orbit moves each description below its heading. At (380px) and below, header page links disappear and the smallest text/artifact arrangements tighten further.

The fixed navigator remains above the bottom edge and has a maximum width of `calc(100vw - 1.5rem)`. On mobile it respects the safe-area inset, hides its introductory label, and shows the active world’s name alongside all five numbers. Footer bottom padding (7rem) provides space for this control.

## Elevation & Depth

Most content is flat. Fine rules, tonal section changes, type scale, and negative space provide structure. Depth appears in the dimensional crimson knot, Orbit’s layered spherical linework and dots, Studio’s rotated paper objects, and the floating layout navigator. The shared navigator uses a small ambient shadow (`0 8px 30px #0003`) and a (16px) backdrop blur. Studio’s ticket and note share a paper shadow (`4px 14px 24px #7b25252b`). Studio’s recruitment step sheets add a paper shadow (`0 14px 24px #4b1a271c`). Ordinary articles and FAQ rows do not lift.

Motion supports each world through native-scroll scenes and bounded responses. Signal’s sculpture enters once over (1.2s), then shifts with scroll as its geometry rotates; its three manifesto lines travel in alternating horizontal directions. Edition’s poster hinges in perspective with scroll. Orbit’s globe rotates and tilts with scroll, with a bright traveller tracing an inclined orbit; direct dragging and its bounded damped release remain available. Studio’s paper ticket and note rotate in opposing directions, and its ticker travels horizontally and reverses when scrolling reverses; the small typographic stamp remains static. Invitation’s large seal geometry rotates and its lettering grows slightly with scroll, while the closing seal stamps into place once over (650ms). Revealed prose moves (22px) over (600ms), and buttons respond over (200ms) with a short arrow movement over (300ms). The common finite-motion easing is `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`. Scroll updates are smoothed, scoped to visible scenes, and settle to rest without an idle animation loop.

Reduced motion disables the knot entrance, scroll-scene transforms, closing-seal stamp, and poster hover rotation, makes transitions effectively immediate, restores instant anchor scrolling, and prevents prose reveals and globe release inertia. The globe retains direct drag and arrow-button control without scroll rotation. Reduced transparency makes the navigator opaque and removes its blur. Increased contrast strengthens shared text and rule tokens.

## Shapes

Signal’s application button is a full pill; Edition and Studio use square buttons; Orbit uses a small radius; Invitation uses a nearly square radius. Header application anchors remain pills except Studio’s square treatment. The shared navigator and topic chips are capsules, while the globe controls and closing seal are circles. These are intentional differences, recorded in `rounded` and component tokens.

The geometric vocabulary is fine angular linework with concentric circles, a six-ray asterisk, simple single-stroke arrows, small Orbit nodes, and Studio’s dashed ticket perforation. The geometry and icons are inline SVG. The text wordmark is the implemented mark; there is no supplied official image logo in this build. The sole raster image is `/images/crimson-knot.webp`, a transparent (1100 × 1100) crimson ribbon sculpture used only by Signal. It has descriptive alternative text, explicit dimensions, responsive sizing, and priority loading. The other worlds build their visual interest in HTML, CSS, SVG, and canvas.

## Components

### Buttons

`ApplyLink` is an anchor to the current Google Forms destination, with a trailing diagonal SVG arrow. It has a minimum height of (54px), padding from the button component tokens, font size (.85rem), weight (600), and a gap of (2.4rem). Invitation uses (.78rem) text and a (1.5rem) gap. The color and corner shape come from the world-specific button tokens. Edition’s final reversed panel uses a cream button with wine text.

On hover, the button changes to its theme treatment and its arrow moves (2px right, 2px up). Links and buttons compress to (.97) on press. Every keyboard-focusable element has a visible (2px) current-color outline offset by (6px). The header join anchor scrolls to the final action; the final application anchor opens the configured form in the same tab.

### Navigation

The header combines the text wordmark, expanded club/university name when space permits, three local anchor links, and the join anchor. On mobile the middle local link is hidden; the smallest breakpoint hides all local links. A focus-only skip link leads to `main`.

The fixed five-world navigator is a named navigation landmark. Each route link includes a visible number, a descriptive accessible label, and `aria-current="page"` on the active world. Its selected capsule reverses to light paper and dark ink. Hover adds a subtle translucent fill. Route prefetching is disabled for these comparison links. Desktop controls are at least (40px) tall and mobile controls (44px).

### Native details

FAQ rows use native `details` and `summary`, fine top/bottom rules, a minimum question height of (78px), and a small CSS plus sign whose vertical stroke flattens when open. Supporting answers are muted, constrained to (65ch), and retain normal document flow. Mobile questions are at least (72px) tall. Invitation places the same FAQ in its reading column with smaller type. Studio’s discipline list also uses native details, larger typography, ruled rows, and an arrow that turns on expansion. These controls remain usable without JavaScript.

### Ruled content and paper artifacts

Journey articles use a top rule, tabular two-digit step, concise title, and supporting copy; the desktop three-column row becomes numbered stacked rows on mobile. Edition’s four editorial articles are open columns with top rules, an asterisk, serif title, tags, and body copy. They are not raised cards. Studio’s ticket and note are deliberately rotated paper artifacts with their own ambient shadow, compact lettering, and a dashed perforation.

### Recruitment steps and MIBC events

`ClubProgram` follows the hero on Signal, Edition, Orbit, and Studio. Invitation places it first in the paper column beside the invitation panel. Two labelled sections show the user-specified recruitment sequence—Register, Book an appointment, Check your mail—and four researched MIBC events: MarXcellence, Innovade, Amelior, and Intra-Ignite. The event descriptions are evergreen summaries supported by `docs/event-sources.md`; there are no seasonal dates or outdoor-program claims. Hults is not presented as an MIBC event.

Only Register has an underlined “Start your application” anchor to the intentionally placeholder `https://forms.google.com`. The appointment and mail steps end in explanatory paragraphs separated by a fine rule; these are not controls and do not imply working booking or email delivery. Every event has a source link: MarXcellence to `https://marxcellence.com/`, Innovade to `https://nsumibc.org/innovade/`, Amelior to the official NSU newsletter PDF, and Intra-Ignite to `https://nsumibc.org/intra-ignite/`. The Amelior link explicitly says “Read about Amelior (PDF)”.

The default recruitment layout has three equal columns, (370px) minimum sheet height, (2rem) padding, and a (22px) folded corner. Signal uses separated cream, rose, and wine sheets. Studio uses the same separated sheets with a paper shadow. Edition uses adjoining transparent ruled panels and serif titles. Orbit uses transparent top-ruled panels, small accent dots, and staggered vertical positions. Invitation stacks compact sheets with serif titles in the reading column. Registration and event links have a (44px) minimum height and inherit visible keyboard focus.

Events use a ruled list beside sticky decorative geometry in Signal and Orbit. Edition uses a two-column editorial list without the geometry; Studio uses broad rows with category, title, and description columns, placing the source link under the description; Invitation uses a single list below smaller non-sticky geometry. At (760px) and below, headings, recruitment steps, and event lists stack, geometry enters normal flow, Orbit’s offsets are removed, and recruitment sheets remain unrotated. When the viewport is at least (650px) tall and every sheet is shorter than the viewport minus (150px), `PageMotion` enables `mobile-stack`: sheets stick at (20px / 40px / 60px), separated by (24vh), and preceding sheets scale from (1) to (.935) as the next sheet approaches. Scrolling back restores their scale. Edition and Orbit use opaque sheet backgrounds; a sheet with keyboard focus comes to the front at full scale. Short viewports or taller content retain the ordinary stacked flow. Program body copy is (1rem / 1.65), captions are (.8rem), and registration and event links have (48px) minimum targets. The common sections use (6.5rem) vertical padding, reduced to (4rem) on mobile; Invitation uses (4rem) vertically with (10%) inner gutters, reduced to (7%) on mobile.

The track spread and season collection retain their CSS names and use the existing `PageMotion` scene progress. On desktop Signal and Studio fan the outer sheets; Edition turns panels in perspective; Orbit moves the staggered sheets; decorative event geometry rotates; Studio’s alternating event titles shift slightly sideways. On phones, event headings translate from alternating (42px / -32px) offsets, rotate from (5deg / -5deg), and scale from (.88) to (1) as they arrive; the rule traces and asterisk travel also follow scroll progress. These transforms and the mobile recruitment deck are enabled only without a reduced-motion preference and settle with native scrolling. Reduced motion and the default presentation retain static, visible content; content remains visible without JavaScript. The sidecar’s program specimen shows all three recruitment steps and four sourced event articles in static Signal styling; application code supplies route variants and scroll motion.

### Edition poster

The large red cover is a real button with a top and bottom rule, Space Grotesk lettering, and compact editorial captions. Click or keyboard activation cycles three messages. Scrolling hinges the poster around its left edge in perspective. Hover darkens the poster and rotates it slightly; text changes have a short finite entrance. The poster stays readable at its initial state without JavaScript.

### Orbit globe and topic chips

The dotted globe uses a canvas with a static SVG fallback and descriptive image label. Scrolling rotates and tilts it while a bright traveller follows an inclined orbit. Horizontal drag turns it while vertical touch scrolling remains available; two (40px) arrow buttons provide an alternative. Topic buttons are a named group of three capsules, each at least (44px) tall with `aria-pressed`. Selection reverses the chip and updates the adjacent polite live region. The copy region reserves height to reduce layout movement. The globe uses no geographic claims or location pins.

The sidecar includes standalone visual HTML/CSS specimens for the five application-button variants, navigator, native FAQ, poster, topic chips, ticket, and program content. It preserves native details behavior and CSS states. The scroll scenes, canvas renderer, poster message cycle, and topic state changes remain application code rather than being recreated in the specimen panel. No text-input component exists in this project.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** retain each world’s matched palette, typography, composition, and button shape.
- **Do** keep the club identity and application action clear in every world.
- **Do** use fine rules, balanced headings, local fonts, and generous reading space as the common foundation.
- **Do** preserve native details, visible keyboard focus, mobile stacking, and reduced-motion/transparency support.
- **Do** keep motion scroll-controlled or finite and interaction-led, with ordinary content visible before JavaScript runs.

### Don't:

- **Don't** flatten the five worlds into a single burgundy template.
- **Don't** replace the single abstract sculpture with unsupported member photography, testimonials, or invented identity assets.
- **Don't** present the proposal title, unsupplied dates, eligibility, numeric club claims, or endorsements as official facts. Use the researched MIBC event names and evergreen summaries documented in `docs/event-sources.md`; do not restore outdoor descriptions or seasonal dates.
- **Don't** add dependencies, autoplay media, or continuous background animation to reproduce the completed motion.
